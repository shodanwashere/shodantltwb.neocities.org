**This is a backup of a post I made on LinkedIn in case I get censored there.**

Lately, I've been hearing more and more frequently the argument that "AI (more specifically, LLMs) enable people to do things and complete tasks they couldn't do before."

What, like drawing? Coding? Writing?! You learn to compose in 2nd grade. Before anyone destroys me for "gatekeeping", I want to give you a reality check.

AI isn't enabling you, it's cutting you short because you wanted a shortcut. Every time you use it to complete a task, you miss a crucial moment to learn and improve as an individual. Not only that, LLMs aren't even that great. They're nothing more than "Token Predictors". If you don't know what that means, then you should read a bit. Not that you'll listen to me, you'll just ask your AI to summarize it all for you.

From what I've gathered, Alex Volkov coined the term prompt kiddie back in 2023 but it wasn't until last year that I saw someone actually trying to describe what a "prompt kiddie" is, namely, Terry Reinert, CEO of Fleet Defender. Just like them, everyone still tries to soften the blow. Apparently, a prompt kiddie just isnt good enough at prompting the AI. Let me shatter your illusions.

A prompt kiddie isn't good at whatever they're prompting the AI for either. They've disregarded the process of learning to make room for "just getting the job done". They don't understand the nuances behind drawing, video making, photography, writing prose, writing code, and crucially, they do not understand how the AI's answers are generated and why, depending on the context, that can be problematic. Same as a script kiddie. They just like that there's tools to get to the result they want. They don't care how it works, only that it does, and it sets anyone with this mentality up for failure.

Every time I explain this concept, people get mad and say I am gatekeeping, that AI "enables people to do things they couldn't do before". This kind of infantile, imature attitude really just reminds me of "Spider-Man: Homecoming", of that very important quote that Tony Stark tells Peter Parker. "If you're nothing without this suit, then you shouldn't have it." A lot of people need to realize that the bar for these very human centric activities has been lowered so far down that anyone can pick it up and start learning. That's what people are mostly afraid of: learning. Because learning means facing failure, and a lot of people have faced so many failures, they'd rather cheat to just have one win.

What you'll realize, sooner or later is that learning how to do it by yourself will make you realize that AI is just a crutch, and the gratification you will feel from seeing the results of your effort and reaping the fruits of your labor is worth infinitely more than whatever soulless output ChatGPT gave you for a logo to put on wine bottles.

Earn the tool, and maybe you'll realize you don't even need it at all.
